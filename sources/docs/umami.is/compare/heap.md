## Intentional tracking, not auto-capture noise

Heap's auto-capture approach sounds appealing in theory — track everything automatically. In practice, users report that it creates a flood of event noise that makes finding relevant data difficult and time-consuming. Umami takes the opposite approach: you track what matters with a clean, intentional setup. One script tag gives you pageviews, referrers, devices, and locations immediately, and custom events are simple to add when you need them.

## Affordable analytics at any scale

Heap's pricing jumps dramatically past its limited free tier, with no affordable mid-tier option between free and enterprise. Umami offers transparent pricing starting with a free tier, paid plans from $20/month, and self-hosting that's always free. You shouldn't need an enterprise budget to understand your website.

## Independent and actively maintained

Since its acquisition by Contentsquare, Heap users have reported declining support quality and uncertainty about the product's direction. Umami is an independent, open-source project under the MIT license. You can self-host for complete control, inspect every line of code, and rely on an active community. No acquisitions, no surprises, no vendor lock-in.

## Privacy-first by design

Heap tracks individual users and builds detailed behavioral profiles, which creates GDPR compliance challenges that require careful configuration. Umami collects no personal data, uses no cookies, and is GDPR and CCPA compliant out of the box. You get the website insights you need while fully respecting visitor privacy — no consent banners, no compliance headaches.

## Frequently asked questions

Is Umami a good alternative to Heap?

Yes. Umami covers events, funnels, cohorts, segments, session replay, and heatmaps — plus the acquisition side: traffic, referrers, and campaigns — with real-time data instead of Heap’s delayed processing, and transparent pricing instead of contact-sales tiers.

Does Umami auto-capture every event like Heap?

No — by design. Heap’s auto-capture records everything, which produces noisy data that needs constant cleanup. Umami uses intentional tracking: you define the events that matter, so your data stays clean and meaningful.

How does Umami pricing compare to Heap?

Heap’s free tier is limited and paid plans require talking to sales, with enterprise features paywalled. Umami Cloud is free to start with paid plans from $20/month, with funnels, cohorts, and revenue reports on every plan — and self-hosting is completely free.

Is Umami data real-time?

Yes. Umami’s dashboard updates in real time, while Heap processes data with delays of up to several hours. If you need to see the impact of a launch or campaign immediately, Umami shows it as it happens.
