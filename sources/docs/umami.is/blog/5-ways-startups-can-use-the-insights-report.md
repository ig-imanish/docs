# 5 Ways Startups Can Use the Umami Breakdown Report

October 22nd, 2024Posted by Nick Andrews

![5 Ways Startups Can Use the Umami Breakdown Report](https://umami.is/images/blog/image-breakdowns.webp)

The Breakdown report allows you to slice into your website and app analytics via advanced filtering. Filtering options include URL, Page title, Referrer, Query, Browser, OS, Device, Region, City, and Host. The Breakdown report will then take that sub-set of data and break it down by Views, Visits, Visitors, Bounce rate, and Visit duration.

The magic with the Breakdown report is that you can combine multiple filters to get a granular look at things. That enables founders and product teams to uncover hidden patterns, identify key trends, and make data-driven decisions to improve performance.

Let's explore five practical ways startups can use the Umami Breakdown report to drive growth and optimize their user experience.

## Breakdown Report Primer

Before diving into specific use cases, let's briefly cover how to use the Breakdown report:

1. In your Umami dashboard, navigate to the Reports section and create a new Breakdown report (the Breakdown report comes standard with all versions of Umami).
2. Select your website and date range.
3. Select the fields you want to analyze (e.g., URL, Page title, Referrer, OS, Device, Country).
4. Run the report.

For detailed instructions on setting up and using the Breakdown report, please refer to our [documentation](https://umami.is/docs/reports/report-breakdown). For a deeper walkthrough of every field, metric, and filter operator, see [Understanding the Breakdown Report](https://umami.is/blog/understanding-the-insights-report).

Now, let's explore five ways startups can use the Breakdown report to drive growth and make data-driven decisions.

## 1\. Optimize Your Content Strategy

For startups focused on content marketing, or those with content-rich products, the Breakdown report shows which content actually resonates with users.

Segment your data by URL or Page title to identify which pieces of content perform best in terms of views and engagement. Apply filters to focus on high-value metrics like visit duration, bounce rate, or queries (which can capture UTM parameters in URLs).

Suppose you're a SaaS startup that regularly publishes blog posts related to your product. You might discover that your how-to guides and product comparison articles consistently receive more views and have lower bounce rates than your company news updates.

You can dig deeper by comparing bounce rates and visit durations across content types. If long-form, in-depth articles have lower bounce rates and longer visit durations than shorter, more general posts, your audience values detailed, comprehensive content.

Adjust your content strategy accordingly:

- Focus more resources on creating in-depth guides and comparison articles
- Experiment with repurposing successful content into different formats (e.g., turning popular blog posts into infographics or videos)
- Tailor your content calendar to prioritize the topics and formats that resonate most

## 2\. Refine Your Acquisition Channels

For startups focused on growth, understanding which acquisition channels are most effective is crucial. The Breakdown report helps you dig past raw traffic volume and into traffic quality.

Use the Referrer field to segment your data by traffic source. This goes beyond knowing which social platform or search engine sends you traffic — you can drill down to specific referring URLs. You might discover that a guest post you wrote for an industry blog is driving a significant amount of highly engaged traffic.

Combine this with metrics like bounce rate, pages per session, and conversion events to assess quality. Say you're running campaigns on both LinkedIn and Meta. The Breakdown report might reveal that while Meta drives more overall traffic, visitors from LinkedIn have a lower bounce rate and higher average visit duration — suggesting LinkedIn is bringing in more qualified leads.

You can then optimize your marketing efforts:

- Allocate more budget to channels that bring in highly engaged visitors
- Refine your strategies on underperforming channels to attract more qualified leads
- Double down on successful tactics, such as guest posting or industry partnerships
- Use UTM parameters to track specific campaign performance and iterate in real time

## 3\. Sharpen Product-Market Fit and User Segments

For startups, achieving product-market fit is a major milestone. The Breakdown report's segmentation capabilities help you understand who your users actually are and refine your offering.

Combine fields like Device, OS, and Country to create specific user segments, then analyze how these segments differ in engagement, feature usage, or conversion rates. You might find that mobile users from a particular country have a much higher conversion rate for a specific feature — an insight that could lead you to optimize the mobile experience for that market or build targeted campaigns around that segment.

Device and operating system data also gives you clues about who your users are and how they access your product. If you're a B2B SaaS startup and notice a significant portion of users accessing your web app from mobile devices, that might indicate a need for a more robust mobile experience.

The report can also surface opportunities for new features:

- Analyze the most frequently visited pages or commonly used features
- Identify pages or features with high drop-off rates that might need improvement
- Use these insights to guide user interviews or surveys for deeper understanding

For example, say your startup offers a project management tool and you notice users frequently visit the calendar view but drop off from that page at a high rate. That suggests users value the calendar but the current implementation isn't meeting their needs — a strong prompt for focused user interviews.

## 4\. Diagnose User Experience Problems

The Breakdown report provides several ways to gather data that can inform your UX decisions.

One application is comparing user behavior across devices. You might notice that mobile users have a higher bounce rate on certain pages compared to desktop users, indicating those pages aren't optimized for mobile.

Imagine your startup offers online coding courses. Using the Breakdown report, you notice that while your lesson pages have good engagement on desktop, mobile users drop off quickly. Digging deeper, you find that mobile users spend very little time on pages with embedded code editors. That suggests your code editor isn't mobile-friendly, leading you to either improve its mobile functionality or take a different approach for mobile learners, such as video.

Bounce rate and visit duration also help you identify pain points more generally. Pages with high bounce rates or low visit durations may indicate usability issues or misaligned expectations. If your pricing page has a high bounce rate, it could suggest sticker shock or that the pricing information isn't clear enough.

The same data can guide feature and navigation decisions:

- Look at the most common user paths to understand how people navigate your product
- Optimize your menu structure and internal linking based on these insights
- Watch for features that are rarely discovered, which may need better promotion or placement

For instance, you might find that Android users heavily use a collaboration feature while iOS users rarely touch it — informing both your roadmap and your educational content.

## 5\. Plan Geographic Expansion

If your startup has ambitions of geographic expansion, the Breakdown report can inform those decisions.

By analyzing location data, you can understand how your product or website performs in different regions. Let's say you're a U.S.-based e-commerce startup selling home goods. Through the Breakdown report, you notice significant traffic from Canada, and those visitors have higher-than-average engagement. That could indicate an opportunity for expansion into the Canadian market.

Dig deeper by comparing bounce rate and visit duration across locations. You might find that visitors from certain cities or regions spend more time on your site and bounce less, helping you identify promising markets for targeted campaigns.

The report can also help you tailor strategies by region:

- Feature certain products more prominently for visitors from high-interest regions
- Assess the performance of localized versions of your website or product
- Inform decisions about where to focus marketing spend or allocate support resources
